# About

<p class="intro-highlight">I am a PhD candidate in computational materials science and a teaching fellow in the Department of Mechanical and Materials Engineering at Queen's University. I use density-functional theory, molecular dynamics, and kinetic Monte Carlo to study how defects form, segregate, and move in engineering alloys. I am a developer of <a href="https://github.com/hugomoison/pyKMC" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">pyKMC</a>, an off-lattice kinetic Monte Carlo code, and <a href="https://github.com/Kerrator/pylatkmc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">pylatkmc</a>, an on-lattice one.</p>

<div class="bio-section">
<h2>Research</h2>

My first PhD paper examined the third-element effect in Ni–Cr–Al: how chromium changes the vacancy concentration and the segregation of aluminum to grain boundaries. Both affect oxidation resistance at high temperature. This work is published in <em>Scripta Materialia</em> (<a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scriptamat.2026.117203" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">2026</a>). I am now working on modelling dealloying, both to understand corrosion in nuclear materials and for catalysis. I have built an on-lattice kinetic Monte Carlo model that works in tandem with an off-lattice one, to give better fidelity at longer time scales. Earlier research explored ion-pair trapping by cyclic molecules for energy storage and light detection. See <a href="/research">Research</a> for details.

</div>

<div class="bio-section">
<h2>Methods</h2>

I run molecular dynamics and nudged elastic band calculations in LAMMPS with embedded-atom potentials, and kinetic Monte Carlo in pyKMC and pylatkmc. I analyze the results in Python. The full list of tools is on the <a href="/research#methods--tools">Research</a> page.

</div>

<div class="bio-section">
<h2>Teaching</h2>

At Queen's I teach MECH 479 Nanomaterials Science &amp; Engineering and co-teach MECH 203 Mathematical and Computational Tools for Mechanical Engineers II. Before that I was a teaching assistant at Queen's and coordinated laboratory sections for first- and second-year engineering and science courses at Ontario Tech. My teaching emphasizes reproducible computation, hands-on problem-solving, and responsible use of AI tools in scientific work. See <a href="/teaching">Teaching</a> for course details.

</div>
