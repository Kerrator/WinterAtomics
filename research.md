# Research

I study atomic-scale mechanisms in alloys using density-functional theory, molecular dynamics, and kinetic Monte Carlo. My work connects defect behaviour, segregation, and transport to how materials perform in service.

## PhD Research

<div class="research-card">
<h3>Modelling Dealloying with Kinetic Monte Carlo <span class="status-badge active">Active</span></h3>
<p><em>PhD, Queen's University</em></p>
<p>Modelling dealloying, both to understand corrosion in nuclear materials and for catalysis. I have built an on-lattice kinetic Monte Carlo model, <a href="https://github.com/Kerrator/pylatkmc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">pylatkmc</a>, that works in tandem with the off-lattice code <a href="https://github.com/hugomoison/pyKMC" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">pyKMC</a>, to give better fidelity at longer time scales. Animations of surface events from a pyKMC run in Ni–Cr–Al are on the <a href="/pykmc/">pyKMC page</a>.</p>
</div>

<div class="research-card">
<h3>Third-Element Effect in Ni–Cr–Al <span class="status-badge past">Published</span></h3>
<p><em>PhD, Queen's University</em></p>
<p>Examined how chromium changes the vacancy concentration and the segregation of aluminum to grain boundaries in Ni–Cr–Al. Both affect oxidation resistance at high temperature. The simulations used an embedded-atom potential in LAMMPS: nudged elastic band for migration barriers, molecular dynamics for diffusion, and hybrid Monte Carlo for grain-boundary segregation.</p>
<p>Published in <em>Scripta Materialia</em> 277 (2026) 117203: <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scriptamat.2026.117203" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">article</a>, <a href="/assets/pdfs/kerr-2025-scripta-cr-vacancy.pdf">preprint</a>.</p>
</div>
