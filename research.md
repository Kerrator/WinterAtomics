# Research

I study atomic-scale mechanisms in alloys and related systems using density-functional theory and molecular dynamics. My work connects defect behaviour, segregation, and transport properties to how materials perform in engineering applications.

## Current Research

<div class="research-card">
<h3>Chromium Effects in Nickel-Based Alloys <span class="status-badge active">Active</span></h3>
<p><em>PhD, Queen's University, 2024–present</em></p>
<p>Investigating how chromium influences vacancy concentration and grain-boundary aluminum segregation in Ni–Cr–Al alloys using DFT and molecular dynamics. This work addresses fundamental questions about oxidation resistance in high-temperature superalloys and the interplay between alloying elements, point defects, and grain-boundary chemistry.</p>
<p>Published in <em>Scripta Materialia</em> 277 (2026) 117203: <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scriptamat.2026.117203" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">article</a>, <a href="/assets/pdfs/kerr-2025-scripta-cr-vacancy.pdf">preprint</a>. Walkthrough videos from my talk on PyKMC (Ni–Cr–Al kinetic Monte Carlo) are on the <a href="/pykmc/">PyKMC page</a>.</p>
</div>
